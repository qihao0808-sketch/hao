About this portfolio

This portfolio was built for GEEC1052 (Effective Communication in English) to document the planning, promotion, and evaluation of the Inter-Campus English Public Speaking Competition 2026, themed "Voices of Tomorrow: Empowering Future Educators through English."

Each section reflects a different register — the formality of an institutional request, the warmth of a message to a friend, the immediacy of a social caption, and the rigour of an evaluative report. The report section additionally documents the five stages of the writing process, from the first rough notes to the polished final draft, so the development of the writing itself remains visible.

02 Drafting — Draft 1
18 July 2026 · First full draft
1.0 Introduction

The Inter-Campus English Public Speaking Competition 2026 was organised on 15 July 2026 at Auditorium Taza, Institute of Teacher Education Batu Lintang Campus. The competition was held under the theme "Voices of Tomorrow: Empowering Future Educators through English." It aimed to improve students' English communication skills, increase their confidence in public speaking, and strengthen relationships among future teachers from different campuses.

This report evaluates the overall effectiveness of the competition by discussing its strengths, weaknesses and possible improvements. It also examines whether the event achieved its intended objectives.

2.0 Overview of the Event

The competition involved approximately 60 participants from five Institute of Teacher Education campuses. The programme included an opening ceremony, a keynote speech, the prepared speech competition, the impromptu speech final, a question-and-answer session, the prize-giving ceremony and a networking tea session.

Overall, the programme was completed successfully and participants actively took part in the activities throughout the day.

Draft weakness — "completed successfully" is too general; no supporting evidence given.
3.0 Strengths of the Event

One strength of the competition was the encouraging level of participation. Around 60 students joined the event, allowing participants from different campuses to meet each other and exchange ideas. This created a positive learning environment for everyone.

Another strength was that participants became more confident in speaking English. During the competition, many contestants delivered their speeches with confidence and received positive responses from the audience. This showed that the event helped students improve their public speaking skills.

Overall, the competition was successful in promoting communication skills and confidence among future educators.

Draft weakness — evidence is vague; no examples or participant feedback; analysis stops at description.
4.0 Areas for Improvement

Although the competition was generally successful, there were several areas that could be improved.

Firstly, the publicity could be better. Most of the promotion was done through social media. Because of this, some students might not have received the information early enough to participate in the competition.

Secondly, time management could be improved. Some activities took longer than expected, so the question-and-answer session became shorter. This reduced the opportunity for participants to interact with the judges.

Lastly, the judging criteria were not explained clearly before the competition. Some participants were unsure about how the final scores were determined.

Draft weakness — mostly descriptive; no discussion of consequences.
5.0 Critical Discussion

The competition achieved many of its objectives. Most participants had the opportunity to speak English confidently and communicate with students from other campuses. This supports the idea that public speaking activities can help students improve their confidence.

However, there were still some weaknesses. The limited promotion may have reduced participation from certain campuses. In addition, the shorter question-and-answer session limited communication between judges and participants.

According to Zhang and Hu (2022), participation is important in helping learners build confidence. The competition generally achieved this objective because students were given opportunities to speak before a large audience.

Garret (2024) also mentions that communication involves listening and understanding. Since the Q&A session was shortened, participants had fewer opportunities to communicate with the judges.

Overall, the competition was meaningful, although several aspects could still be improved.

Draft weakness — theory mentioned but not critically applied; stakeholder perspectives missing.
6.0 Recommendations

Several improvements can be made in future competitions.

Firstly, publicity should begin earlier so that more students can receive information about the event. Different platforms such as Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp should also be used.

Secondly, the judging rubric should be shared with participants before the competition starts. This can help them understand how they will be assessed.

Finally, the programme schedule should be improved so that more time can be allocated for the question-and-answer session.

Draft weakness — recommendations are practical but lack justification and expected outcomes.
7.0 Conclusion

In conclusion, the Inter-Campus English Public Speaking Competition 2026 was a valuable learning experience for future educators. The event successfully encouraged English communication, confidence and interaction among students from different campuses. However, improvements in publicity, time management and judging transparency are still needed. With better planning, the competition can become more effective and provide greater benefits for future participants.

An Evaluative Report on the IPG Inter-Campus English Public Speaking Competition 2026

Prepared by: Wong Qi Hao, Chairperson, Organising Committee
Programme: PPISMP Semester III, BC (SJK)
Institute: Institute of Teacher Education Batu Lintang Campus
Date: 19 July 2026
1.0 Introduction

The IPG Inter-Campus English Public Speaking Competition 2026 was organised on 15 July 2026 at Auditorium TAZA, Institute of Teacher Education Batu Lintang Campus, under the theme "Voices of Tomorrow: Empowering Future Educators through English." The competition aimed to improve English communication skills, strengthen public speaking confidence, and promote meaningful collaboration among future educators from different Institute of Teacher Education campuses.

As effective communication is an essential competency for future teachers, public speaking competitions provide authentic opportunities for participants to develop confidence, critical thinking, and professional communication skills. Therefore, this report evaluates the overall effectiveness of the competition by analysing its strengths, identifying areas for improvement, and determining whether the event successfully achieved its intended objectives. The evaluation is supported by direct observations, participant feedback, and the evaluation criteria established for this report.

2.0 Overview of the Event

The competition involved approximately 60 participants representing five Institute of Teacher Education campuses. The programme consisted of an opening ceremony, a keynote address, the prepared speech competition, the impromptu speech final, a question-and-answer session, the prize-giving ceremony, and a networking tea session.

Overall, the event was conducted according to the planned programme, with active participation from contestants, judges, lecturers, and audience members. The variety of activities created opportunities not only for competitive speaking but also for professional interaction and knowledge sharing among future educators.

3.0 Strengths of the Event

One significant strength of the competition was its encouraging level of participation. Approximately 60 students from five Institute of Teacher Education campuses participated in the event, creating valuable opportunities for future educators to exchange ideas, establish professional relationships, and collaborate across campuses. This level of participation contributed meaningfully towards the competition's objective of strengthening inter-campus networking through authentic communication experiences. Beyond the competition itself, participants were able to broaden their professional networks and learn from peers with different educational backgrounds.

Another notable strength was the improvement in participants' confidence when speaking English. Throughout the competition, many contestants maintained eye contact, responded confidently during the impromptu speech session, and interacted effectively with both the judges and audience members. Informal feedback collected after the event indicated that several participants felt more confident expressing their ideas before a large audience compared to previous speaking experiences. These observations suggest that the competition enhanced participants' communication, presentation, and public speaking skills.

Taken together, the event created an engaging learning environment that encouraged participants to communicate confidently while promoting collaboration among future educators.

4.0 Areas for Improvement

Although the competition achieved many of its objectives, several aspects could be improved to enhance its overall effectiveness.

Firstly, the publicity strategy could have been more comprehensive. Promotion relied mainly on social media platforms, which may not have reached all potential participants in a timely manner. Consequently, some campuses were represented by fewer participants than expected, reducing the diversity of representation and partially limiting the competition's objective of promoting wider inter-campus collaboration.

Secondly, improvements in time management are necessary. As several earlier programme segments exceeded their allocated time, the question-and-answer session had to be shortened. As a result, participants had fewer opportunities to explain their ideas, respond to judges' questions, and receive constructive feedback. This reduced the educational value of one of the competition's most interactive components.

Finally, the judging criteria were not explained in sufficient detail before the competition began. Although the judging process was conducted professionally, several participants were uncertain about how marks were awarded. Greater transparency would improve participants' understanding of the assessment process and strengthen confidence in the competition's fairness.

5.0 Critical Discussion

Overall, the competition achieved its primary objectives of improving English communication skills, increasing public speaking confidence, and promoting interaction among future educators. However, a closer evaluation shows that the extent to which these objectives were achieved varied across different aspects of the programme.

One of the competition's core objectives was to provide an inclusive platform where students of varying proficiency levels could gain confidence through authentic speaking experience. This objective was clearly reflected in the competition, where participants from different campuses were given equal opportunities to present their ideas in a supportive environment. The high level of participation demonstrated that the event created meaningful opportunities for students to develop confidence through authentic communication experiences.

However, the objective of promoting inter-campus collaboration was only partially achieved. While students who attended benefited from networking opportunities, the limited publicity strategy may have reduced participation from several campuses. Consequently, fewer representatives from certain campuses meant fewer opportunities for broader collaboration and the exchange of diverse perspectives.

Meaningful communication involves not only the delivery of a message but also thoughtful interaction and responsiveness to how it is received. Because the question-and-answer session was shortened, participants had limited opportunities to clarify their ideas, respond to feedback, and engage in deeper communication with the judges. As a result, the competition placed greater emphasis on speech delivery than on interactive communication, slightly reducing its educational impact.

From the perspective of different stakeholders, the competition produced varying outcomes. Participants gained valuable speaking experience and increased confidence, while audience members benefited from observing effective public speaking techniques. The organising committee successfully coordinated a large-scale inter-campus event but could further improve publicity planning and programme scheduling to maximise participation and learning opportunities.

On the whole, the competition proved instrumental as a platform for developing communication skills. Nevertheless, addressing the identified limitations would enable future competitions to achieve their objectives more comprehensively.

6.0 Recommendations

Several practical improvements are recommended for future competitions.

Firstly, publicity should begin at least six weeks before the competition and utilise multiple communication channels, including Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, printed posters, faculty announcements, and campus ambassadors. This strategy would improve awareness across participating campuses, encourage wider student participation, and allow sufficient time for registration and preparation.

Secondly, the judging rubric should be published before the competition and explained during the participants' briefing. A short feedback session after the announcement of results would also enable contestants to understand their strengths and areas for improvement while increasing transparency and trust in the assessment process.

Thirdly, the programme schedule should be revised by allocating a dedicated time slot for the question-and-answer session rather than integrating it into the presentation schedule. This would provide participants with more opportunities to engage in meaningful discussions with judges, receive constructive feedback, and further develop their communication skills.

Finally, future competitions could incorporate reflective sessions or networking workshops following the competition to encourage continued collaboration among participants from different campuses. Such activities would reinforce the competition's educational objectives beyond the formal contest itself.

7.0 Conclusion

The IPG Inter-Campus English Public Speaking Competition 2026 was, on balance, a worthwhile educational initiative — one that enhanced English communication skills, public speaking confidence, and professional interaction among future educators, while also surfacing clear, fixable gaps in publicity, scheduling, and assessment transparency. Its value lies not only in what it delivered this year, but in what it makes possible for future editions: a more inclusive, better-paced, and more transparent platform for pre-service teachers to find their voice in English.
